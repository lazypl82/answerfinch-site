Google Forms email notifications not working? What to check

Someone filled in your form, but no email arrived. Go through these checks in order. Start with the notifications built into Google Forms. If you use Answerfinch, continue with the second part.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Google Forms' own notifications

You can receive an email for each new Google Forms response. Check these first.

  1. Is the setting on for your account? In the form, open Responses, then More (three dots), and check Get email notifications for new responses. Only the person who turns it on gets these emails. If a teammate also wants them, they need to turn it on in their own Google account.
  2. Are you looking in the right inbox? You get the email in the Google account you used to turn on the setting. If you use more than one Google account, check the other one.
  3. Is it in spam or filtered? Search your mail for the form's name, including spam and any folders your filters move mail to.
  4. Did the respondent expect a copy? A copy for the person who filled in the form is a different setting. Open Settings, then Responses, and look at Collect email addresses and Send responders a copy of their response. See Google's help page for both.

With Google's setting, you get an email for every response. If you want an email only for certain answers, or want it to go to someone else, you can use an add-on such as Answerfinch.

Answer-based emails with Answerfinch

Start with the send log. Open your form, open Answerfinch from the add-on menu (puzzle piece icon), and go to Send log. Choose Refresh.

You can get a summary of recorded failed or unconfirmed sends at your own address, at most once in 24 hours. To send it, you need a later form submission or sidebar visit, the required Google permissions and enough sending quota. Delivery isn't guaranteed. In the summary, you see counts and reasons, without form answers or recipient addresses. To turn these emails off, open Answerfinch and go to Details.

If there is no entry for the response

  1. Right Google account. You see your rules only in the Google account you used to create them. Open the form while signed in to that account.
  2. Alerts are on. At the top of the sidebar, you should see Alerts on for this form.
  3. The rule is active. On Free, you can run one rule at a time across your account. Check that you've selected the rule you want.
  4. The answer matches the rule. For a text question with the is condition, check capital letters and spaces: the whole answer must match. For contains, check that the answer includes the text you entered. If an answer doesn't match the rule, you won't send an email for it. You might see no log entry for that response.
  5. Alerts need reconnecting. If prompted, choose Reconnect alerts. You won't automatically send emails for earlier responses after reconnecting.
  6. The response is old. You won't send an email automatically once the response is 48 hours old.

If there is an entry

  • SentGoogle accepted the email for sending. You can't tell from this whether it reached the inbox, so ask the recipient to check spam.
  • FailedFor Failed, you see a reason when one is available, such as reaching your Google account's daily sending limit.
  • UnknownGoogle didn't confirm whether it was sent. It may already have gone out.
  • ProcessingThe send is still in progress. Refresh again in a moment.

For eligible Failed or Unknown entries, you can use Review and resend. If you resend an Unknown entry, the person may get it twice. You see a warning first. When you resend, you use the current rule and response, not a saved copy of the original email.

Google's daily sending limit

You send from your own Google account, so Google's limits for that account apply. Google limits how many recipients you can email each day. Personal Gmail accounts have a lower limit than Google Workspace accounts. Google lists the current numbers on its quotas page. In Answerfinch, open Plan, then Details and data. Under Sending limits, check Google's remaining recipients and the observation time. Test emails count too.

Emails to the person who filled in the form

For a confirmation email, choose where the recipient's address comes from: Google Forms' collected email or an email question. If email collection is off, select the question that contains their email address.
